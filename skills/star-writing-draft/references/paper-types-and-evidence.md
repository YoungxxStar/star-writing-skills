# Paper Contributions and Publication Artifacts

Identify the paper's purpose and dominant contribution before choosing its
argument spine. The patterns below describe contribution logic, not mandatory
publication templates. Combine them when the work has more than one genuine
contribution.

`Primary` refers to the paper itself. `Supporting` refers only to an appendix,
supplement, response, or release artifact permitted for the target publication
stage. Do not invent a supporting artifact when the target does not use one.

## Method, algorithm, or technique

- Center the unresolved technical requirement and the insight behind the design.
- Explain why each consequential choice follows from that insight.
- Use matched comparisons, strong alternatives, ablations, failure cases, and resource costs to separate explanations.
- Keep the central mechanism and decisive evaluation in the paper.

## Theory or formal analysis

- Center the question, assumptions, definitions, statements, and proof dependencies.
- Distinguish exact results, approximations, conjectures, and empirical illustrations.
- Keep theorem scope and the proof idea in the paper.
- Move routine lemmas and extended derivations only when supporting material is
  permitted and the paper remains checkable.

## Empirical discovery or observational study

- Center the scientific question, measurement validity, population or regime, and competing explanations.
- Make sampling, uncertainty, confounding, and robustness visible.
- Keep the primary result and its identifying evidence in the paper.
- When permitted, use supporting material for extended subgroup analyses,
  alternative specifications, and diagnostics.

## Experimental study

- Center the hypothesis, intervention, controls, measurement, and uncertainty.
- Distinguish planned analyses from post-hoc interpretation.
- Keep the primary comparison and key controls in the paper.
- When permitted, use supporting material for full protocols, calibration,
  secondary outcomes, and additional controls.

## Dataset, benchmark, instrument, or research resource

- Center the scientific need, construction validity, coverage, intended use, and measurable questions.
- Demonstrate what the resource enables beyond describing its scale.
- Keep validity evidence, representative content, splits, and strong baselines
  in the paper.
- When permitted, use supporting material for schemas, collection details,
  exhaustive statistics, and usage guidance.

## System, application, or translational paper

- Center the domain requirement and the adaptation needed to satisfy it.
- Evaluate end-to-end utility as well as component behavior.
- State operating conditions, failure modes, costs, and deployment assumptions.
- Keep evidence of actual utility in the paper; move operational detail only
  when supporting material is permitted and the detail does not determine the
  claim.

## Qualitative or mixed-methods study

- Center the research question, sampling rationale, analytic procedure, reflexivity, and evidentiary chain.
- Connect themes and interpretations to traceable observations.
- Preserve representative evidence and the main analytic logic in the paper.
- When permitted, use supporting material for instruments, coding detail,
  extended excerpts, and additional triangulation.

## Review, synthesis, or position paper

- Center the organizing question and the logic of inclusion, comparison, or argument.
- Separate sourced findings from the authors' synthesis and recommendations.
- Make selection boundaries and counterpositions explicit.
- Keep the synthesis logic in the paper. When permitted, use appendices for
  search strategies, coding schemes, and extended inventories.

## Interdisciplinary paper

- Define concepts at the boundary between communities.
- State which contribution belongs to which field and which evidence each community requires.
- Avoid treating one field's testbed as proof of another field's broad claim.
- Keep the cross-field contract and shared scientific conclusion in the paper.

## Associated publication artifacts

- **Rebuttal or response:** map each comment to its premise, response, supporting
  evidence, resulting change, and unresolved point.
- **Supplement or appendix:** add derivations, protocols, extended results, or
  reproduction detail without carrying an unstated premise required by the
  paper.
- **Revision or cover letter:** map each material change or editorial obligation
  to the affected manuscript location and supporting evidence.
- **Code, data, or artifact release:** state the exact availability and
  reproduction level supported by the delivered package.

## Primary-to-supporting allocation test

When the target permits supporting artifacts, keep an item in the primary
deliverable when removing it would prevent the intended reader from:

- understanding the question or contribution;
- checking a headline claim;
- interpreting a number, figure, or comparison;
- seeing a decisive control or limitation;
- understanding why the method or analysis is valid.

Move an item to a supporting artifact only when it expands confidence, coverage,
or reproducibility without carrying the central inference.
