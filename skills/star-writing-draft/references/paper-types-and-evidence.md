# Paper types and evidence architecture

Identify the paper's dominant contribution before choosing its argument spine. Combine patterns when the work has more than one genuine contribution.

## Method, algorithm, or technique

- Center the unresolved technical requirement and the insight behind the design.
- Explain why each consequential choice follows from that insight.
- Use matched comparisons, strong alternatives, ablations, failure cases, and resource costs to separate explanations.
- Keep the central mechanism and decisive evaluation in the main text.

## Theory or formal analysis

- Center the question, assumptions, definitions, statements, and proof dependencies.
- Distinguish exact results, approximations, conjectures, and empirical illustrations.
- Keep theorem scope and the proof idea in the main text.
- Move routine lemmas and extended derivations only when the main argument remains checkable.

## Empirical discovery or observational study

- Center the scientific question, measurement validity, population or regime, and competing explanations.
- Make sampling, uncertainty, confounding, and robustness visible.
- Keep the primary result and its identifying evidence in the main text.
- Use the supplement for extended subgroup analyses, alternative specifications, and diagnostics.

## Experimental study

- Center the hypothesis, intervention, controls, measurement, and uncertainty.
- Distinguish planned analyses from post-hoc interpretation.
- Keep the primary comparison and key controls in the main text.
- Use the supplement for full protocols, calibration, secondary outcomes, and additional controls.

## Dataset, benchmark, instrument, or research resource

- Center the scientific need, construction validity, coverage, intended use, and measurable questions.
- Demonstrate what the resource enables beyond describing its scale.
- Keep validity evidence, representative content, splits, and strong baselines in the main text.
- Use the supplement for schemas, collection details, exhaustive statistics, and usage guidance.

## System, application, or translational paper

- Center the domain requirement and the adaptation needed to satisfy it.
- Evaluate end-to-end utility as well as component behavior.
- State operating conditions, failure modes, costs, and deployment assumptions.
- Keep evidence of actual utility in the main text; move operational detail only when it does not determine the claim.

## Qualitative or mixed-methods study

- Center the research question, sampling rationale, analytic procedure, reflexivity, and evidentiary chain.
- Connect themes and interpretations to traceable observations.
- Preserve representative evidence and the main analytic logic in the main text.
- Use supplementary material for instruments, coding detail, extended excerpts, and additional triangulation.

## Review, synthesis, or position paper

- Center the organizing question and the logic of inclusion, comparison, or argument.
- Separate sourced findings from the authors' synthesis and recommendations.
- Make selection boundaries and counterpositions explicit.
- Keep the synthesis logic in the main text; use appendices for search strategies, coding schemes, and extended inventories.

## Interdisciplinary paper

- Define concepts at the boundary between communities.
- State which contribution belongs to which field and which evidence each community requires.
- Avoid treating one field's testbed as proof of another field's broad claim.
- Keep the cross-field contract and shared scientific conclusion in the main text.

## Main-to-supplement allocation test

Keep an item in the main text when removing it would prevent a reader from:

- understanding the question or contribution;
- checking a headline claim;
- interpreting a number, figure, or comparison;
- seeing a decisive control or limitation;
- understanding why the method or analysis is valid.

Move an item to supplementary material when it expands confidence, coverage, or reproducibility without carrying the central inference.
