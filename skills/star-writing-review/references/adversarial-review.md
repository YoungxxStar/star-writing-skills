# Adversarial Reviewer Simulation

Use this workflow for consequential reviewer simulation, rejection-risk
analysis, or a disputed central contribution. The purpose is to find the
strongest true objection, not to manufacture a harsh review.

## Run a two-sided analysis

### 1. Steelman the paper

Reconstruct the strongest version supported by the available sources:

- the bounded research question;
- the closest legitimate alternative;
- the central contribution without promotional labels;
- the decisive evidence and what it isolates;
- the established scope, limitations, and practical or theoretical value.

Do not attack an interpretation that the paper does not need to defend.

### 2. Reduce the contribution to plain operations

Describe the work without its proposed names or rhetoric. Ask whether the
contribution reduces to added information, capacity, tuning, data, scale,
selection, a known composition, or a different evaluation. Treat this as a
hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

### 3. Build the strongest plausible rejection case

Write one concise rejection rationale, then make its chain explicit:

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| Target claim | Which central proposition would fail? |
| Factual premise | What verified fact must be true for the objection to hold? |
| Rival explanation | What simpler account could produce the same result? |
| Decisive test | What evidence would distinguish the accounts? |
| Current evidence | Does the study control, measure, or leave the rival open? |
| Consequence | Does the issue invalidate, narrow, or merely obscure the claim? |

Attack the earliest scientific or evidential fracture. Do not inflate a local
notation, reporting, or style problem into a fatal scientific objection.

### 4. Construct the strongest author response

Answer the rejection case using only verified definitions, sources, analyses,
and results. Test whether the response:

- directly contradicts the objection's factual premise;
- excludes the rival explanation through design or evidence;
- supports a narrower but still material contribution;
- relies only on intention, terminology, rhetoric, or future work.

If the evidence defeats the objection, reject the objection and state the
supported result without reflexive weakening. If the response needs missing
evidence, the objection remains open even when the proposed mechanism is
plausible.

### 5. Adjudicate

Classify the rejection case as:

- **decisive:** invalidates a central claim or required study contract;
- **substantive but repairable:** needs a bounded control, analysis, source, or
  claim revision;
- **presentation-induced:** the evidence is adequate but the paper invites a
  material misreading;
- **unsupported:** the objection depends on a false premise or an irrelevant
  demand;
- **unresolved:** the responsible source is unavailable or conflicting.

Recommend the smallest repair that resolves the identified risk. Do not request
generic additional experiments or broader scope.

## Enforce objective judgment

- Apply the same evidential standard to the paper and its alternatives.
- Separate verified facts, reasoned inferences, suspicions, and unavailable
  evidence.
- Report strong evidence as clearly as weak evidence; skepticism is not a
  reason to understate an identified result.
- Preserve negative or limiting evidence even when it weakens the preferred
  story.
- Ignore authority, confidence, and tone when judging a comment or rebuttal.
- Distinguish a scientific defect from a reporting omission and a prose
  preference.

## Return the decision

Report:

1. the steelmanned contribution;
2. the one-sentence strongest rejection case;
3. the strongest evidence-grounded author response;
4. the adjudication and remaining uncertainty;
5. the smallest required repair and whether wording alone can provide it.
